# BONFIRE simulation report

Generated by `npm run simulate`. Do not edit by hand. Every number comes from the deterministic simulator in `packages/simulator`, run for 60 days at seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. Values are mean ± standard deviation across seeds.

These are assumption-driven scenarios, not forecasts of price or adoption. Rounds run every 3 days and resolve on their start day, so overlapping rounds are not modeled here. The contract's rule math is shared with the Rust tests through fixtures.

## Recommended rule values

| Rule | Value | How it was chosen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `up_bps` / `down_bps` | 10500 / 8500 (×1.05 / ×0.85) | Lowest score in the controller sweep, among pairs whose implied success rate is 60–90% |
| `budget_bps` | 2500 (25%) | Fewest rounds skipped once stoking stops; near-ties go to the share that pays creators more |
| `target_multiple` | 4 | Typical accepted payout closest to half the budget |

Threshold floor, starting threshold and minimum budget stay at 1,000 EMBER, 10,000 EMBER and 1 NEAR. The sweeps did not vary them.

- **The candle cuts sniping.** Snipers decided the winner in 0.7 rounds with a candle close, against 11.1 with a hard close.
- **A whale still dominates.** With 300,000 EMBER, one wallet ends with 47% of all Ash. Amount weighting does not stop this.
- **Faucet farming drains the pool.** 60 farming wallets empty the faucet around day 2 and hold 34% of all Ash.
- **Splitting does hide concentration.** Ash Gini is measured per wallet, so the same whale reads 0.52 as one wallet and 0.33 across ten.
- The ties scenario has only 10 wallets, so its top-10 share is 100% by construction.

**Where a strategy beats the design:** the candle-waiter group pays least per win, at 4,502 EMBER against 4,846 for early backers, over 11.1 and 13.7 wins. Waiting into the candle is a modest edge on cost that gives up some wins.

**Where it does not:** snipers burned 7,840 EMBER and had 410,977 returned over 0.9 wins. Under a candle close, most last-moment commitments never count.

The chosen pair scores 0.825, and the next eligible pair scores 0.854. The two are close, so the choice is a judgment call rather than a clear win.

**A whale can push the threshold down.** A saboteur abstains until the threshold is half its starting value, then wins as cheaply as it can. At the chosen values the threshold falls to about 3,705 EMBER, and the saboteur wins 8.1 of 20 rounds at 2,802 EMBER per win, against 13.0 wins at 17,192 for a whale that backs every round. That is a 84% discount on the cost of a win. It works only where the crowd alone cannot reach the threshold.

## Strategy note

- **Late commitments.** Before the draw nobody knows the deciding checkpoint, so a commitment at the last checkpoint counts only if the draw lands there. Waiting cuts exposure to a burn and also cuts the chance of affecting the result.
- **Free-riding.** Lower supply benefits every holder, whether they burned or not. What a burner alone gets is direction over the mission, a larger commission for it, and Ash. Whether those are worth the cost is a question about real people, and this model cannot answer it.
- **The cost of winning.** Only counted EMBER on the accepted winner burns. Losing and late EMBER returns. Backing a popular mission is cheap per round won, and swinging a close round costs up to the whole gap to the leader.

## Model limits

Backers follow fixed behaviors with random mission preferences and do not learn or react to price. Real attendance, creator quality and whale behavior are unknown. Testnet NEAR has no market value.
